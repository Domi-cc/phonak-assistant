# Measurements, 27 November 2026

First day on the real hardware. Everything here comes from the app's own journal and from the
phone's system log. Device addresses and names are left out.

**Set-up:** Google Pixel 10 Pro XL, Android 17 (build `CP3A.261005.005`, SDK 37.1). Phonak Audéo
EON R, right ear only. Shelly BLU RC Button 4 (`SBBT-004CUS`, no Zigbee), battery 100 %. The app
held the assistant role and its voice-interaction service was bound in every run below. Phone
and remote were on a desk or on the body, never more than about a metre apart. The phone was
reached over Wi-Fi debugging in the later runs and was not charging.

Sample sizes are small. Read the numbers as "this worked", not as rates.

## Hearing-aid tap

| What | Result |
|---|---|
| The double tap arrives as a hands-free voice-recognition request | yes, every time (system log: `startVoiceRecognitionByHeadset`) |
| Android then starts an activity with `VOICE_COMMAND` | within about 60 ms |
| Same with the phone locked and the display off | yes; no screen wake-up in the log |
| No default handler set, phone locked | the chooser is created and finishes itself 40 ms later; nothing audible |
| Google's handler as default, phone locked | opens the voice link, closes it again after about 5 s |
| A second tap within 5 s of an unanswered one | rejected by Android ("already pending") |
| A tap during a running session | arrives as "stop" |

The link runs in wideband quality (16 kHz); super-wideband was not negotiated.

**The failure at 15:56:40** was a bug in the first build. The app asked for the audio route
itself (`AudioManager.setCommunicationDevice`) while the voice-recognition session was being set
up. The audio server then tried to open the link as a "virtual call", the Bluetooth stack ended
the voice recognition (`voice recognition is still active, just called stopVoiceRecognition`)
and rejected the incoming audio connection. Since the app no longer requests the route in a
voice-recognition session, the failure has not come back, and `stopVoiceRecognition` at the end
of a session returns true.

## Shelly remote

**What it sends.** Service data for UUID `0xFCD2`, for example `44 00 5e 01 64 3a 01 3a 00 3a 00
3a 00`: device information `0x44`, packet id, battery, then one button object per button at a
fixed position. Seen so far: `01` press, `02` double, `03` triple, `04` long, `80` hold.

**Best case** (16:49, app on the screen, fastest scan mode, passive scan type):

| What | Result |
|---|---|
| Events | 10, packet ids 93 to 102 without a gap |
| Missed | 0 |
| Burst of a normal press | 22 to 32 packets received over 950 to 1020 ms (about one packet every 39 ms) |
| Burst of a hold | 44 packets over 1530 ms |
| Signal | −37 to −56 dBm |

**In a session, phone locked, display off, in Doze** (scan mode "balanced", started from the
session's foreground service; the session was opened by a hearing-aid tap):

| Session | Events received | Missed (from packet-id gaps) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16:54:14 to 16:56:18 | 17 | 1 | the gap was between two presses of the same button four seconds apart |
| 16:59:02 to 16:59:23 | 4 | 0 | ended by a long press of button 4 |

In this mode 8 to 16 packets of a burst arrive (one hold: 34 packets over 2348 ms). Delivery
from the radio to the app took 2 to 51 ms.

**Not measured yet:** reception without an open session (the phone listening all day), the hold
gesture held for ten seconds, anything with distance or a body in between, and the effect of
Android 17's scan allowance (the phone has the flag set; whether it bites is unknown).

"Hold" and "long" are two different events. Once a hold was followed three seconds later by a
long press of the same button, once it was not. What exactly the remote does while a button
stays down is open.

The announcement goes over the media channel and is only spoken if Android would send it to the
selected hearing aid and nowhere else.

## Other observations

- After an app update signed with the same key the system rebinds the assistant service within
  a second, also with the phone locked, dark and in Doze. Role, permissions and the "Always"
  choice for the voice button survived every update.
- Installing the app (a new handler for `VOICE_COMMAND`) made Android drop the earlier "Always"
  choice for the Google app, as predicted.
- The speech engine produced all 38 phrases for the remote in 26 s in the background.
- Wi-Fi debugging connected without pairing, because the phone already trusted the computer's
  key from an earlier USB session.
