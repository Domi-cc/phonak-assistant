# Does a sound channel from the phone get in the way of the hearing aid's own job?

Date: 2026-11-28. Nothing in this report was run on a device, and nobody listened with the aid on the ear. Every statement is a lead until the tests in section 5 have been done.

The owner asked for a check: the app must not stop what the hearing aid is there for, amplifying the surroundings. Can that happen, for example because a sound channel from the phone stops the amplification?

How this report was made: three reports written on that day, each then re-checked by a second agent ("the skeptic") whose only job was to refute it against the sources.

- **The app:** an audit of every Kotlin file under `app/src/main` and `core/src/main` at commit `cfaccde` (build 20, tag `apk/20`). Code reading only. Changes made after that commit are not described here.
- **The hearing aid:** Phonak documents (user guides, product information, fitting guides, knowledge base) and forum reports from wearers.
- **The phone:** the Android 17 source at tag `android-17.0.0_r1` and developer.android.com.

This document is the condensed result. Where a skeptic corrected a report, the corrected statement is given and the correction is named.

Status marks on every statement:

- **[V]** verified in a primary source (Phonak or Sonova document, developer.android.com, AOSP source, the app's own code). The source is given.
- **[C]** community report (forum). A hint, not a fact. Almost all of them are about older Phonak generations.
- **[I]** inferred from verified sources, stated by none of them.
- **[U]** unknown: no source found. Needs the device or the ear.

After the mark comes the skeptic's verdict: *confirmed*, *overstated*, *unsupported*, *wrong*, or *not re-checked*. Statement numbers: H for the hearing aid, A for Android, P for the app's paths, R for the app's risks.

Words used here, for a reader who knows Android but not hearing aids:

- **Hearing program:** the processing the aid applies to its own microphones. Normally an automatic program (Phonak: AutoSense OS) that classifies the surroundings and picks a class such as "Calm Situation" or "Speech in Loud Noise".
- **Streaming program:** the program the aid runs while it receives sound over Bluetooth. A different program with its own settings. "+ mic" in its name means the microphones are mixed in.
- **Fitting:** the settings an audiologist stored in the aid with Phonak's fitting software, Phonak Target. **myPhonak** is the wearer's phone app.
- **Media channel:** Bluetooth A2DP, one direction, used by the app's unprompted announcements. **Voice link:** the hands-free (HFP) audio link, two directions, used by the app's sessions.
- **Generations**, oldest first: Marvel, Paradise, Lumity, Infinio, Infinio Ultra, EON. The owner's aid is an Audéo EON R (one aid, right ear).

In one paragraph: the app does not touch the amplification, but it does make the aid change program, for about 6 to 7 s per announcement and for about 33 s or more per remote session (about 13 s for an echo test). Phonak says the microphones stay on by default while the aid receives streaming signals, and describes the surroundings as still there during a call; it names no level for either. How loud and how differently processed the surroundings are in that time is decided in the aid, by a fitting nobody outside the audiologist's screen can read. Only a test on the ear can tell for this aid.

How to read this: sections 0 and 5 are enough for the question. Sections 2 to 4 are the evidence, statement by statement, for whoever wants to check or change something.

## 1. The question, and what documents can and cannot tell

What can go wrong for the wearer has three parts, and each has a different owner:

1. **How long a channel is open.** Decided by the app (when it starts and stops playing) and by Android (when it starts and suspends the Bluetooth stream). Readable in code. Sections 3 and 4.
2. **What the aid does while a channel is open and how long it needs to come back.** Decided in the aid, by its firmware and by the fitting. Section 2.
3. **How that sounds.** Only on the ear. Section 5.

Limits of the sources:

- **Phonak** describes behaviour almost only in its knowledge base, whose articles carry no date and mostly name no generation. The EON documents (user guide 029-1462, product information 027-0742-02, datasheet, the paper on AutoSense OS AI 8.0) name programs and features but describe no behaviour while streaming. The fitting guides that show the relevant settings are for Target 11.0 and 11.2, written before the EON. The EON needs Target 12, and no Target 12 guide is public. No Phonak source gives a level, a default or a time.
- **Forum reports** are about Marvel, Paradise, Lumity and Infinio aids. One report about an EON turned up, and it is not about amplification.
- **Android:** the only public Android 17 platform source is the first release (tag `android-17.0.0_r1`, branches `android17-release` and `android17-security-release`). The phone runs a newer build (`CP3A.261005.005` by [`../measurements/2026-11-27.md`](../measurements/2026-11-27.md)), its Bluetooth stack is an updatable module, and the Pixel's audio HAL is closed. No device tree of the Pixel 10 is public.
- **The app:** code reading. The measurement notes of 27 November say nothing about how the aid sounded during any run.

Not opened or only read second-hand: the phonakpro.com page that defines "Environmental balance" (redirects to a landing page, no archived copy found); the phonakpro stethoscope PDF (HTTP 503; the knowledge base article that links to it was read instead); the German EON user guide (not read); the English Hearing Review article on AutoSense OS 3.0 (only through a summarising reader, quotes shortened; its content was confirmed against Phonak's French edition of the same paper).

## 2. The hearing aid's side

Sources are Phonak's own unless marked [C]. "KB" is the Phonak knowledge base under `https://www.phonak.com/en-us/support/knowledge-base/articles/`. All pages were fetched on 2026-11-28 by the report's author and again by the skeptic.

The skeptic's verdict on the hearing-aid report as a whole: its main sentence (the microphones stay on) is real and nothing contradicts it, but its summary rounded up in six places. All six are applied below: the program of a voice-recognition link is not known (H2.4); "the audiologist can set 0 dB" rests on one forum post and did not help in another (H1.5); for the call program every source that names a default says minus 6 dB (H2.3); "about three seconds" to come back is the low end (H5.2); the sentence about lost adjustments was quoted from the wrong line (H3.2); and "stay on by default" says nothing about level, about this fitting or about the EON (H1.1).

That the surroundings are "still amplified" was the report's reading, not Phonak's wording.

### 2.2 The hands-free link

- **H2.1** [V] *confirmed*. Generation: KB not named; program name EON; structure Marvel. A hands-free link has the highest priority in the aid. The EON has a program "Phone call + mic" for calls, listed outside the automatic classification (H1.2).
  - "Bluetooth phone calls (HFP) have always the highest priority and a calling notification will be heard." (KB as H1.1)
  - A Phonak course handout on AutoSense OS 3.0 (2019-04-15, Marvel), slide "With Marvel - Classification of streamed input signals", shows "HFP Phone calls" outside the box of classified inputs: https://www.audiologyonline.com/files/event/32900/32913/contphoankautosenseos3-0.pdf
  - Skeptic: none of these sources says that a voice-recognition link without a call uses this program. See H2.4.
- **H2.2** [V] *confirmed*. Generation: KB not named; user guide EON. During a call the surroundings can still be heard, and the aid's microphones also feed the phone.

- **H2.3** [C] *confirmed*. Generation: Paradise, Lumity. In the call program the microphones can be attenuated in Target but not switched off, and every source that names a default for it says minus 6 dB. No source reports 0 dB for the call program.

### 2.3 What the wearer and the audiologist can change

- **H3.1** [V] *confirmed*. Generation: myPhonak 7.5 guide (2026-06); EON product information for the feature. "Ambient balance" (German app text "Umgebungsbalance"; in Phonak's feature tables "Environmental Balance") shifts the focus between the streamed signal and the surroundings. It is marked "non-standard feature", depends on how the aid is programmed, and is offered while streaming. The EON product information lists Environmental Balance on all levels.
  - "Depending on how the hearing aids are programmed and which wireless accessories you have, you can adjust more settings, such as Tinnitus noise and Ambient balance, if you are streaming."
  - myPhonak 7.5 user guide V13.00/2026-06, page 12 (URL under H1.6); German edition: https://www.phonak.com/content/dam/celum/phonak/master-assets/de/documents/apps/myphonak/ph-user-guide-my-phonak-7.5-v13.00-de.pdf
  - Skeptic: a forum wearer confirms that the option disappears when the stream closes. Phonak's examples are the TV Connector and music; nothing says the slider is offered in the call program.
  - It can be reached only while the stream runs, which is too short for an announcement of a few seconds.
- **H3.2** [V] *confirmed*. Generation: none named, except that the article singles out Marvel. Every stream is a program change, and adjustments made in myPhonak that were not saved as a custom program are lost after it. Afterwards the most recently used program comes back (on Marvel: the base program).
  - "Each time when the sound is streamed to the hearing aids, the active program will switch to Bluetooth streaming program. After the streaming, the last used hearing program will be activated, and the adjustments not saved as a custom program will be lost." https://www.phonak.com/en-us/support/knowledge-base/articles/myphonak-app-does-not-keep-my-settings (German: https://www.phonak.com/de-de/support/knowledge-base/articles/myphonak-app-does-not-keep-my-settings )
  - "When the hearing aids receive an audio signal from the smartphone a streaming program will be activated automatically. When the streaming is finished, the program that was most recently used will be activated." https://www.phonak.com/en-us/support/knowledge-base/articles/hearing-aids-switch-program-when-receiving-a-sound-notification
  - Consequence for the app: each hourly announcement, calendar reminder and session throws away unsaved volume or sound adjustments. Phonak's remedy is to save them as a custom program.
  - Skeptic: to call this certain for the EON is a step beyond the text, which names no generation. An Infinio wearer describes the volume staying changed after a stream (H8.3), so the return is not always clean.
- **H3.3** [C] *confirmed*. Generation: Lumity (2023 to 2024), one post of December 2025. The Ambient balance choice is not kept: wearers set it again at each connection, the audiologist can set the starting mix for media in the fitting software, the app cannot store it.

Answer: "For music / audio, it can be set to 100% audio via the audiologist software but not the myPhonak App. For phone calls, it can't be done." https://forum.hearingtracker.com/t/bluetooth-default-ambient-balance-and-muting-for-phonak-l90s/81717
  - "almost every time I'm streaming (at least the first time I use that kind of streaming since booting the aids) I'm hitting that button", https://forum.hearingtracker.com/t/increasing-streamed-signal-with-tv-connector-using-target/109327
- **H3.4** [V] *confirmed*. Generation: Target 11.0 and 11.2 guides, before the EON. The fitter can change the streaming programs separately from the acoustic ones: fine tuning of "AutoSense OS (streaming)", program options per program, and the behaviour of button and tap in the situations Standard, Streaming and Phone call. A choice of how streaming is activated (automatic, manual, manual with beep) exists only for TV Connector, Roger and PartnerMic.
  - "Click [AutoSense OS] to modify all acoustic automatic programs or [AutoSense OS (streaming)] to modify automatic streaming programs. ... Program options can be adjusted from the default settings. The features can be activated, deactivated, or changed in strength for each program individually. ...   - Target 11.0 Step-by-step Guide 029-1387-33 (URL under H1.3); Target 11.2 User Guide V2.00/2025-10: https://www.phonak.com/content/dam/celum/phonak/master-assets/en/documents/resources/phonak-target/target-11/ph-user-guide-target-11.2-92x125-en.pdf
  - Skeptic: that the automatic switch for Bluetooth phone audio cannot be made manual follows from its absence in that list; a forum wearer says the same ("you can't turn off Autoswitching").

### 2.4 The automatic program during a stream

- **H4.1** [V] *confirmed*. Generation: Marvel documents; EON product information for the program list. Streamed sound is part of the automatic system: it is classified as speech or music and the matching media program is chosen. Phone calls and RogerDirect are not classified.
  - "Phonak Audéo Marvel with AutoSense OS 3.0 now incorporates streamed inputs into the automatic classification process" (Hearing Review article and Insight paper, see H1.7). Fact sheet: "Requires no additional input data beyond ambient or streamed audio." https://www.phonak.com/content/dam/celum/phonak/local-assets/ca/documents/PH_FactSheet_ML-AutoSenseOS_210x297_EN.pdf.coredownload.pdf
  - Speculation, marked as such by the skeptic: the app's chime might be classed as music and its speech as speech, so one announcement could pass through two media programs. By H1.5 (a) the microphone value is shared between the two.
- **H4.2** [I] *confirmed*. While a stream is on, the acoustic automatic program (for example Speech in Loud Noise with its directionality and noise reduction) is replaced by the streaming program with that program's own microphone and noise settings. So for those seconds the wearer most probably does not have the speech-in-noise processing, even if the microphone level is not lowered at all. No Phonak text states this consequence in one sentence.
  - Basis: H3.2 ("the active program will switch to Bluetooth streaming program"); H3.4 (streaming programs are separate entries with their own options); Target 11.0 guide: "Motion Sensor Hearing ... only activates when the AutoSense OS classification is Speech in noise or Speech in loud noise and spheric speech in Loud Noise."
  - Skeptic, making it firmer for earlier generations: the Target 11.0 guide also says Dynamic Noise Cancellation "is featured in Speech in Noise and Speech in Loud Noise programs only" and StereoZoom 2.0 is "active only in the Speech in loud noise program"; the 2019 Marvel article says the microphone input during streaming keeps "the frequency response of a Calm situation". Limit: on the Audéo E-R the class Speech in Loud Noise exists on the Premium level only (H1.2), so the example fits only that level. Which level this aid has is not known to this report.
- **H4.3** [U] *confirmed*. Generation: EON. AutoSense OS AI 8.0 holds the Speech in Loud Noise program more persistently than Infinio did. Whether the classifier keeps its state across a stream, so that the aid returns straight into that program, is not documented: the paper contains no word about streaming. If the state is lost, the aid needs time after each announcement to find the noise program again.
  - "the exit threshold is set below the entry threshold, creating hysteresis, meaning a larger, sustained acoustic change is required to exit the program than to enter it"
  - Phonak Insight "AutoSense OS AI 8.0", 028-2928-02/V1.00/2026-08: https://www.phonak.com/content/dam/phonak/en/evidence-library/white-paper/technical-paper/PH_Insight_AutoSenseOS8.0_210x297_EN_028-2928-02_V1.00.pdf
  - Skeptic: applies to the E-R Premium level only.

### 3.1 The tail: the media stream outlives the last sound by 3 s

- **A1.a** [V] *confirmed*. The output thread goes to standby 3 s after its last active track by default. The system property `ro.audio.flinger_standbytime_ms` overrides the default. `Threads.cpp:274-284`: `static const nsecs_t kDefaultStandbyTimeInNsecs = seconds(3);`
  - Skeptic, two precisions: the 3 s run from the last mixed buffer (`Threads.cpp:5569`), not from the removal of the track, and the property is read once per start of the audio server. The property is not in the public Pixel 9 trees; its value on this phone is unknown.
- **A1.b** [V] *confirmed*. For an output routed to an A2DP device the standby delay is forced to at least 3 s. The property can make the tail longer on the media channel, never shorter. `Threads.cpp:3798-3812` (`PlaybackThread::cacheParameters_l`): "make sure standby delay is not too short when connected to an A2DP sink to avoid truncating audio when going to standby."
  - Skeptic: holds for mixer outputs, which is where the chime and the speech engine's speech land. The clamp is computed again whenever the output's device changes (`Threads.cpp:888-898`, `943-945`). It does not hold for direct and compressed-offload outputs (`Threads.cpp:7361-7375`; `201`: `kOffloadStandbyDelayNs` is 1 s). See A3.a.
- **A1.c** [V] *confirmed*. During those 3 s the mixer keeps writing zeros to the audio HAL, so the A2DP stream carries silence; then it calls the HAL's standby. The stream does not end with the last word. `Threads.cpp:4207-4219`, `5523-5527`, `5576-5623`: "If no tracks are ready, sleep once for the duration of an output buffer size, then write 0s to the output". What the Pixel's offload path puts on the air in that time is not readable.
- **A1.d** [V] *confirmed*, for the AOSP HAL and stack only. In AOSP nothing delays the suspend further: the Bluetooth audio HAL turns standby into a suspend of the stream at once, and the Bluetooth stack turns that into an AVDTP SUSPEND at once (also for a stop request). No suspend timer in `btif_av.cc` or `btif_a2dp_source.cc`. Sources: `hardware/interfaces` `audio/aidl/default/bluetooth/StreamBluetooth.cpp:243-247` and `DevicePortProxy.cpp:617-655`; `packages/modules/Bluetooth` `system/audio_hal_interface/aidl/a2dp/bluetooth_audio_port_impl.cc:111-125`, `a2dp_aidl_transport.cc:87-108`, `system/btif/src/btif_a2dp_source.cc:377-389`, `system/btif/src/btif_av.cc:2610-2642`, `system/bta/av/bta_av_api.cc:271-292`; legacy HAL `system/audio_bluetooth_hw/stream_apis.cc:257-282`.
  - Skeptic, a detail: the stack stops sending media packets at the request and reports "stopped" only when the aid has answered; the HAL waits up to 4.5 s for that answer (`DevicePortProxy.cpp:139`). - **A1.f** [V] *confirmed*. A stopped track leaves the active list once its frames are consumed (about the output latency). ### 3.2 The start of the stream

- **A1.e** [V] *confirmed*. The stream starts as soon as a track becomes active on a sleeping output; the content is not looked at. A track of pure silence starts it, and so does a track whose volume is forced to zero (a muted stream, or playback muted by the Android 17 background rules). `Threads.cpp:5576-5623` (the "anticipated start" branch writes zeros while a track is enabled), `6085-6096`: `if (track->isPlaybackRestricted() || track->getPortMute()) { v = 0; }`; `StreamBluetooth.cpp:154-169`. No silence detection on PCM was found in `btif_a2dp_source.cc`, `btif_av.cc` or the HAL files.
  - Consequences: the announcer's 450 ms of silence before the chime opens the stream by design, and turning the media volume to zero does not keep a sound from opening the stream. Skeptic: that the Pixel's closed HAL or DSP also starts the stream for pure zeros is inference.
- **A1.g** [I] *confirmed*. The chime and the speech from the engine's process are one A2DP stream if the speech track starts less than 3 s after the chime track ended: standby is per output thread and counts from the last mix, whoever owns the track. At 3 s or more the stream is suspended and started again. A cold speech engine could cause that; the journal field `ms` of `announce_speaking` shows the gap.
  - Skeptic: the gap itself was never measured. The 1.5 s and 1.9 s in the measurement notes count from the request and include the set-up before the chime, so the gap was somewhere between about 0.3 and 1.1 s.
- **A3.d** [I] *not re-checked*. The silent lead-in can be shortened or dropped in the app; the stream then starts with the tone. On the phone side the AOSP HAL holds the first write until the aid has accepted the start, up to 4.5 s (`DevicePortProxy.cpp:139`, `555-600`; `StreamBluetooth.cpp:154-169`), so the phone itself does not cut the beginning in the software path. Whether the aid swallows the first fraction of a second while it switches, and what the offload path does, is not known. The saving is at most 0.45 s out of about 5 s: the tail is the large part and cannot be touched.

### 3.3 The formula for one announcement

- **A2.a** [I] *confirmed*, with one correction. The stream is on from the chime track's start (plus the time the aid takes to accept AVDTP START) until the last mixed audio + 3.0 s (plus the SUSPEND round trip). In journal terms:

  **stream-on time is about (`announce_end` minus `announce_start`) + 3 s**, give or take a few hundred milliseconds.

  With N seconds of speech and a gap g between chime and speech: about 0.73 + g + N + 3 s (the chime is 450 ms silence + 160 ms tone + 120 ms silence, `Announcer.kt:258`, `:307`). The report's examples: about 5 s for "Beende", about 6 s for an hourly announcement of the time. The skeptic's worked figure: an announcement whose audible part, chime tone to last word, is about 3 s holds the stream for about 6.5 s (0.45 s silent lead-in, 3 s of sound, 3.0 s of silence, plus tens to a few hundred milliseconds of signalling); if the 3 s are speech alone, 7 to 7.5 s. More than half of a short announcement's stream time is the silent tail.
  - How sure: the 3 s after the last sound are a firm lower bound for mixed PCM (framework code, read at the release tag, unchanged at the head of the release branch). That it is not more is not sure: the Pixel's HAL between standby and the suspend on the radio is closed, the standby property is unknown and can only lengthen the tail, and any other phone sound inside those 3 s starts them again. A cold engine can make the gap longer than the examples assume. Nothing of this was measured.

### 3.4 Can the tail be shortened?

- **A3.a** [V] *overstated*, corrected here. For ordinary PCM playback there is no supported way for an app to close the A2DP stream sooner: the standby delay is a read-only build property with a 3 s floor on A2DP, `AudioTrack` and `AudioManager` have no standby call, and the suspend switch is an API for the Bluetooth stack. `frameworks/base` `media/java/android/media/AudioManager.java:4112-4121`: `@SystemApi(client = SystemApi.Client.MODULE_LIBRARIES) @RequiresPermission(Manifest.permission.BLUETOOTH_STACK) public void setA2dpSuspended(boolean enable) {`
  - Skeptic: the report said "none" without the limit. Direct, compressed-offload and memory-mapped outputs have other standby rules (0, 1 s, or twice the sleep time; a memory-mapped output goes to standby when its last client stops). Public APIs can ask for such outputs (`AudioTrack.Builder.setOffloadedPlayback` with a compressed format, AAudio low latency), and the Pixel 9 configuration lists `compressed_offload` and `mmap_no_irq_out` among the sources of the A2DP sinks. Whether this phone gives an app such an output to the aid, and whether its HAL then suspends sooner, is unknown. A lead, not a recommendation.
- **A3.b** [V] *confirmed* (source reading only, nobody ran it). An unsupported way exists in AOSP: the public `AudioManager.setParameters` passes its string straight to the audio server, which needs only `MODIFY_AUDIO_SETTINGS` (the app holds it) and does not list `A2dpSuspended` among its reserved keys. In the AOSP Bluetooth audio HAL `A2dpSuspended=true` suspends a started stream at once and then drops all media until `A2dpSuspended=false` arrives. `AudioManager.java:4066-4068`; `frameworks/av` `services/audioflinger/AudioFlinger.cpp:1749-1784`, `1801-1811`; `system/audio_bluetooth_hw/stream_apis.cc:329-347`, `517-519` ("drop data for cases of A2dpSuspended=true / closing=true"); `StreamBluetooth.cpp:281-292`. Skeptic: for an AIDL HAL the framework turns the key into `IBluetoothA2dp.setEnabled(false)` on every module that has that interface (`media/libaudiohal/impl/DeviceHalAidl.cpp:1334-1346`), so on a Pixel it would reach the closed primary HAL.
- **A3.c** [I] *confirmed*. Using that knob would be risky, and this report advises against it: Android's audio service keeps its own record of the suspend state and re-applies it (`frameworks/base` `services/core/java/com/android/server/audio/AudioDeviceBroker.java:1019-1132`), so the two would disagree; if the app died between "true" and "false", media to the aid could stay muted; and the Pixel's closed HAL may ignore or handle the key differently. Skeptic: the risk is larger than stated. The audio service writes `A2dpSuspended=false` only when its own recorded state changes (`AudioDeviceBroker.java:1049-1090`), so after a foreign "true" it would not send "false" by itself until the next voice-link cycle or a restart of the audio server.

### 3.5 The voice link's end

- **A4.a** [V] *confirmed*, for the branch where the Bluetooth stack owns the link. `BluetoothHeadset.stopVoiceRecognition` posts two messages back to back: "voice recognition stop", which sends the unsolicited result `+BVRA: 0` to the aid, and "disconnect audio", which removes the SCO link. There is no timer between or after them. The voice link has no tail: it is the one channel whose closing time the app controls. `android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/hfp/HeadsetService.java:918-983`, `HeadsetStateMachine.java:1016-1028`, `1578-1598`; `system/btif/src/btif_hf.cc:1024-1037`, `1089-1110`; `system/bta/ag/bta_ag_cmd.cc:158`, `1832-1834`; `system/bta/ag/bta_ag_sco.cc:315-333`.
- **A4.b** [U] *confirmed*. If the phone runs with "SCO managed by audio", `stopVoiceRecognition` instead asks the audio service to clear the communication device, and the audio side closes the link. How fast that is, and whether this phone uses that mode, could not be determined. `HeadsetService.java:969-978`, `1386-1397`; `HeadsetSystemInterface.java:388-398` (logs "isScoManagedByAudioEnabled state is: ..." on the phone).
  - Skeptic: it can be settled without the ear. The mode is the flag `android.media.audio.sco_managed_by_audio` and the system property `bluetooth.sco.managed_by_audio` together (`AudioDeviceBroker.java:205-226`; `AudioService.java:5745` prints the flag in `dumpsys audio`). The project's own rule that the link drops within tens of milliseconds once no stream is active on it (KDoc of `AudioLink`) fits the audio-managed mode better than the other; a hint, not proof.
- **A4.c** [V] *confirmed*. While the link is closing, `isAudioConnected` still answers "connected"; the broadcast `ACTION_AUDIO_STATE_CHANGED` with "disconnected" marks the real end and is the better timestamp. `HeadsetStateMachine.java:1655-1657`: "TODO: need BluetoothHeadset.STATE_AUDIO_DISCONNECTING"; `372-389`.
- **A4.f** [V] *confirmed*. While SCO is up, Android routes nothing to A2DP: the A2DP devices are taken out of the device choice and the HAL is told `A2dpSuspended=true`. An announcement cannot start a media stream next to the voice link. `frameworks/av` `services/audiopolicy/enginedefault/src/Engine.cpp:160-166` ("remove A2DP and LE Audio devices whenever BT SCO is in use"); `AudioDeviceBroker.java:1056-1067`; `HeadsetStateMachine.java:1293-1301`.
  - Skeptic: the same code sends media, assistant and notification sounds into the voice link while it is up (`Engine.cpp:445-450`). A notification that arrives in a session is heard in the session; it opens no second channel.
  - From the re-check of the app audit: the suspend that the Bluetooth service itself applies (`HeadsetStateMachine.java:1293-1301`) exists in one of two branches only. With "SCO managed by audio" the Bluetooth service does not connect the audio itself and does not suspend media there (lines 1295-1299). The routing rule in `Engine.cpp` does not depend on that branch.

### 3.6 After a session: the output is re-routed to the aid's media device

This is the skeptic's main finding on the phone side. The report said the opposite.

- **A4.d** [V] for each quoted line, conclusion *overstated*. The report: after SCO has closed, AOSP does not start A2DP by itself; the Bluetooth service lifts the A2DP suspension, the audio service sends `A2dpSuspended=false`, the HAL only moves from "disabled" to "standby", and a stream start is requested only by a write on the output. A start that the aid initiates is suspended again at once. `HeadsetService.java:2217-2233`; `AudioDeviceBroker.java:1049-1090`; `DevicePortProxy.cpp:518-532`; `stream_apis.cc:340-346`; `btif_a2dp_source.cc:337-375`; `btif_av.cc:2334-2378` ("If remote tries to start A2DP when DUT is A2DP Source, then Suspend.").
  - Skeptic: each line is right, but a write is still happening. The session's output thread keeps writing zeros for up to 3 s (A1.c), and Android 17's routing code moves exactly such an output to a new device when the voice link goes away. "A normal session end should leave no media stream behind" is not supported for a phone whose voice output can reach the A2DP device, as on the Pixel 9.
- **A4.e** [U], supporting argument *wrong*, corrected here. Whether this Pixel sends anything to the A2DP device right after SCO closes stays unknown. But the report's argument against it (Android's routing code does not move an output without active clients; `AudioPolicyManager.cpp:8742-8748`, `8369-8375`) is wrong for Android 17, and the lean flips.
  - The function goes on a few lines below the cited ones: if no strategy is active on an output, it takes the route of the strategy that was active within the last 7 s. `frameworks/av` `services/audiopolicy/managerdefault/AudioPolicyManager.cpp:8381-8393`: "if we haven't yet moved to standby, but there are no recent clients, we still re-route that patch, which is important for cleaning stale SCO patches. 7000ms to encompass standby time". The caller then forces the new route. The block is present at `android-17.0.0_r1` and at the head of `android17-release`, absent at `android-16.0.0_r4`. URL: https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/tags/android-17.0.0_r1/services/audiopolicy/managerdefault/AudioPolicyManager.cpp
  - The chain, all read in the source: the voice link goes down; the audio service drops the preferred device of the communication strategy (there is no default for a Bluetooth Classic aid); the policy manager re-evaluates every output; for the session's output the recent strategy is "phone"; with no call and no SCO the phone strategy takes the last connected media device, which is the aid's A2DP device; the patch is installed 100 ms later. `AudioPolicyManager.cpp:4790-4850` (`updateCallAndOutputRouting`, force at 4845), `4877-4908` and `81` (100 ms delay), `8716-8731`; `common/managerdefinitions/src/AudioOutputDescriptor.cpp:95-112`; `enginedefault/src/Engine.cpp:215-216`, `299-314`; `AudioDeviceBroker.java:2436-2470`, `2479-2515`, `2675-2692`.

Expected result: **a silent media stream of about 2.5 to 3 s after every session, starting about 0.1 s after the link went down.** With a separate Bluetooth audio module (the pure AOSP layout) the same code only clears the stale route and no stream starts. The finding holds in both SCO modes of A4.b.

A check without the ear: the code logs at info level; `adb logcat -s APM_AudioPolicyManager` shows "getNewOutputDevices io <n> recent device override <devices>" (`AudioPolicyManager.cpp:8391`) right after a session end, naming the device the output was moved to. Together with the playing-state broadcast (section 3.8) this settles it.

### 3.7 The tap case

- **A5.a** [V] *confirmed*. When the wearer taps during a session, the aid sends `AT+BVRA=0` and the Bluetooth stack disconnects the audio itself, before it tells the app (broadcast `STOP_VOICE_COMMAND`). The app therefore cannot say "Beende" inside the link after a tap. For every other end it already does (`AudioLink.kt:263-272`). `HeadsetService.java:1737-1797` (`stopVoiceRecognitionByHeadset`: `disconnectAudio()` first, then `deactivateVoiceRecognition`); `HeadsetSystemInterface.java:374-381`.
- **A5.b** [I] *unsupported*, corrected here. The report's timeline: after a tap the aid sees the hands-free audio link closing; at least 0.5 s later an AVDTP START (`SessionService.kt:276`, `347-364`: `AFTER_TAP_MS = 500`); a media stream of about 5 s for the chime, "Beende" and the 3 s tail; then AVDTP SUSPEND. Leaving "Beende" out after a tap would be the only way to make it one switch fewer.
  - Skeptic: that ignores the re-route of 3.6, which is even more direct here. The stack drops the link while the app's session track is still playing (`HeadsetService.java:1770-1786`); voice-usage tracks go to the media device when no call is active, so the output, with its active client, is moved to the aid's A2DP device at once (`AudioPolicyManager.cpp:8369-8380`, `4790-4850`; `Engine.cpp:215-216`, `299-314`). By the source the media stream would then start about 0.1 s after the link went down, not 0.5 s or more, and run on through "Beende" plus 3 s as one stream. If that is what the phone does, leaving "Beende" out shortens the stream to about 3 s but does not remove the second switch.
  - Whether the aid treats "voice link closed, media stream opened a moment later" as one stay in a streaming state or as two program switches with a return in between is the aid's side and not known.

### 3.8 What an app can observe

- **A6.a** [V] *confirmed*. `BluetoothA2dp.ACTION_PLAYING_STATE_CHANGED` is public API (added in API level 11, listed for API 37, not deprecated) and needs `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`. It carries the device (`EXTRA_DEVICE`) and, in `EXTRA_STATE`, `STATE_PLAYING` (10) or `STATE_NOT_PLAYING` (11). `BluetoothA2dp.isA2dpPlaying(device)` is public with the same permission and returns the same flag. https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothA2dp ("Intent used to broadcast the change in the Playing state of the A2DP profile."); `packages/modules/Bluetooth` `framework/java/android/bluetooth/BluetoothA2dp.java:93-112`, `602-624`; `framework/api/current.txt:11-16`; `A2dpService.java:615-624`. The skeptic read the raw developer page, not a summary.
- **A6.b** [V] *confirmed*, not run on a device. The broadcast is sent by the Bluetooth app, from its own UID and not the system UID, to every receiver holding `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`. A third-party app receives it with a context-registered receiver flagged `Context.RECEIVER_EXPORTED`; with `RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED` it is dropped. A manifest receiver does not get it. `A2dpStateMachine.java:717-731`; `packages/modules/Bluetooth` `android/app/AndroidManifest.xml:5` (`sharedUserId android.uid.bluetooth`); `frameworks/base` `core/res/AndroidManifest.xml:230`, `services/core/java/com/android/server/am/BroadcastSkipPolicy.java:576-590`, `core/java/android/app/ActivityManager.java:5486-5489`.
  - developer.android.com: "Some system broadcasts come from highly privileged apps, such as Bluetooth and telephony, that are part of the Android framework but don't run under the system's unique process ID (UID). To receive all system broadcasts, including broadcasts from highly privileged apps, flag your receiver with RECEIVER_EXPORTED." https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/broadcasts
  - Skeptic: the hands-free audio broadcast is sent by the identical call (`HeadsetStateMachine.java:384-388`), and the app already receives that one on this phone with `RECEIVER_EXPORTED` (`SessionService.kt:101`). That is the best evidence short of a run. Two details: the manifest receiver is excluded because the intent carries no `FLAG_RECEIVER_INCLUDE_BACKGROUND` (it has only `REGISTERED_ONLY_BEFORE_BOOT`), and the broadcast goes to the Bluetooth app's own user only, which is fine for the primary user.
- **A6.c** [V] *confirmed*. The playing state follows the real AVDTP stream, for sounds of any app: "playing" is reported when the stack enters its Started state, "not playing" when the suspend has completed. It does not depend on software or offload encoding. It therefore includes the 3 s tail and the phone's own sounds, which is exactly what the aid sees. Timestamps carry the broadcast's delivery delay. `btif_av.cc:2573-2583`, `2664-2711`, `3021-3041`; `A2dpNativeCallback.kt:34-46`; `A2dpStateMachine.java:550-573`. Skeptic: it also turns "not playing" when the aid suspends the stream itself, and it would show the stream of 3.6 as well.
- **A6.d** [V] *confirmed*. `AudioManager.isMusicActive` does not show the stream to the aid. It asks whether a track of the music stream type is active right now, on any output, without the standby tail: `return AudioSystem.isStreamActive(AudioSystem.STREAM_MUSIC, 0);` (`frameworks/base` `services/core/java/com/android/server/audio/AudioService.java:4047-4059`). The field `musicActive` in `announce_start` is of little use for this question; the announcer's own tracks are not even on the music stream.
- **A6.e** [V] *confirmed*. `AudioManager.registerAudioPlaybackCallback` shows an ordinary app the active players of all apps, stripped: usage, content type and state remain; the app identity is removed, and on Android 17 the device list is emptied too. It shows tracks, not the stream: no tail, no device. Useful only as a label next to the playing-state events (which kind of sound was active when a stream opened that the app did not cause). `media/java/android/media/AudioPlaybackConfiguration.java:424-464`, `494-511`; `services/core/java/com/android/server/audio/PlaybackActivityMonitor.java:811-822`.
- **A6.f** [V] *confirmed*. For the voice link, `BluetoothHeadset.ACTION_AUDIO_STATE_CHANGED` and `isAudioConnected` are public, need `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`, and are sent the same way. The app journals the broadcast as `hfp_audio_state`, but only while `SessionService` exists (`SessionService.kt:72-101`, `:220`), so phone calls and other apps' use of the link are not in the journal. `framework/api/current.txt:878`, `884`; `HeadsetStateMachine.java:372-389`.
- **A6.g** [I] *not re-checked*. What would let the app log the aid's streaming time: one receiver in the permanent `ListenerService` for both broadcasts (`RECEIVER_EXPORTED`, filtered to the selected aid), each event journaled with the clocks the journal already has and without the address; `isA2dpPlaying` and `isAudioConnected` read once at start for the initial state; the playback callback for usage labels (counts and types, no titles, so they fit the journal's privacy rule). Streams that overlap neither an `announce_start` to `announce_end` window nor a session are the phone's share. At commit `cfaccde` the app has no A2DP proxy and no such receiver.

Where the channels open and close, and what the skeptic added:

- **Media channel.** Opened at `announce/Announcer.kt:274` (the chime's own `AudioTrack`, usage assistant, tied to the aid's A2DP output); the speech follows at `:160` (`tts.speak`, played by the speech engine's process). The checks at `:100-116` (aid selected, connected, Android's output for this usage is the aid's A2DP device and nothing else, engine ready) skip without opening anything (`announce_skipped`). The chime track is stopped and released in a `finally`, at most 780 ms after it started. The speech ends when the engine reports done or error, when the output guard fires, or after 20 s. If the process dies the chime track dies with it, and Android stops the speech of a caller that died (`TextToSpeechService.java:1607-1614`).
- **Voice link.** Opened at `audio/AudioLink.kt:127` (`BluetoothHeadset.startVoiceRecognition`); the session's `AudioTrack` starts at `:144` and plays for the whole session, silence between sounds (rule 5 of the hand-over file). Closed by `AudioLink.close()` (`:259-289`), called from the non-cancellable `finally` in `audio/SessionService.kt:170-195`: "Beende", the end tone, release of the track, `stopVoiceRecognition`. Cancellation (second tap, link lost, focus lost), the atomic start and `onDestroy` all lead there. Sessions end after 30 s without a press (`shelly/RemoteProgram.kt:71`, `:130`), on a long press of button 4, on a second tap, when the link or the audio focus is lost, or at the 5 min limit.

P7: 10 470 ms. Echo inside a session: 9 030 ms. Upper bound of a session: 12 000 + 560 + 300 000 + 530 + 500 + 1 500 = 315 090 ms. Up to 3 s of waiting for the hands-free service come before the request and are not channel time.
- **P1.** The announcement's wake lock (25 s) is taken when `announce()` is called, before up to 2 s of waiting for the hands-free service, up to 6 s for the speech engine and any wait behind another announcement, so it can run out before the 20.78 s do (worst case 28.78 s). The "0.7 to 1.1 s between chime and speech" in the audit is derived from two timings of the older build, not measured. The 20 s cut-off and the guard's question every 200 ms are timers on the main thread, which stand still while the phone sleeps; while sound is playing Android normally keeps the phone awake (general knowledge, not verified on that day).
- **P3.** The trigger is wider than a second tap. The flag is set by the broadcast `STOP_VOICE_COMMAND`, and Android's source sends that broadcast also when the audio link is lost while a recognition is marked as started (`HeadsetService.java:2185-2186`, `:1784`; `HeadsetSystemInterface.java:375-379`). So this extra opening of the media channel can follow a lost link too. By section 3.7 the stream may begin earlier than the app's 500 ms suggest.
- **P5 and P6.** The 30 s idle time is restarted by every input a session takes, not only by announced presses: an echo request from the assist gesture, the screen or adb runs a 9.03 s echo test inside the session and then resets the timer (`shelly/RemoteProgram.kt:87-97`, `:118`; `audio/CountProgram.kt:108-118`, `:128`). The chain of P6 is real but not self-sustaining: only a new press can start the next session. Whether a press can open a session at all with the phone dark is the open question of that build and not measured.
- **P7.** The rule that a tap starts the echo test is at `core/.../input/Routing.kt:108-113` (the audit cited `:119-123`).
- **P9.** Closed by `clearCommunicationDevice` (`audio/AudioLink.kt:278`), with no `stopVoiceRecognition` because none was started. The audit left open whether Android drops the route of a dead client; the skeptic answered it from the source: the audio service removes the route of a client whose process died and stops the voice link when no client is left (`AudioDeviceBroker.java:2417-2425`, `:2601-2604`), and it drops an ordinary app's route after 6 s without playing or recording (`:568`, `CLIENT_INACTIVITY_DEBOUNCE_MS = 6000`). This way of opening the link does not outlive the app's process. By the same source the first start after an unclean end returns false (`HeadsetService.java:790-803`), so a session right after a crash takes exactly this fallback unless the recovery ran first.
- **A trigger nobody had listed.** The tap entry point is an exported activity without a permission (`app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml:72-84`); it has to be, so that Android's Bluetooth stack can start it. Any app on the phone can start it too. Without a device in the intent the check at `trigger/VoiceCommandActivity.kt:40` passes and a voice link to the selected aid is opened.

### 4.4 Where the voice link can stay open longer than meant

1. **The process dies in a session** (crash, update install, kill). Nothing in the process reaches `close()`. Android's Bluetooth service keeps no watch on the caller (no `linkToDeath` or `DeathRecipient` in `HeadsetService.java`). The only repair in the app is `SessionService.recoverAfterCrash` at the next process start (`audio/SessionService.kt:303-313`): if the flag "open" is still set it waits up to 5 s for the hands-free proxy, calls `stopVoiceRecognition` for every connected hands-free device, clears the communication device and clears the flag. Its gaps:
   - If the proxy is not there within 5 s nothing is stopped, the flag is cleared all the same and there is no second try (the journal shows `session_recovered_after_crash` with `voiceRecognitionStopped=null`).
   - The process may have been started by a tap or a press. Recovery and the new session then run side by side, and the recovery's stop can end the new session or make its start be refused (then P9).
   - Found by the skeptic: the recovery writes "not open" unconditionally at its end (`:311`), also when the new session has set the flag at `:160` in the meantime. If the process dies in that session, the next start finds no flag and does not recover.
   - *Wrong* in the audit: "after a force stop by the user nothing restarts the app". For an app that is the selected digital assistant, Android's source says the opposite: when the current assistant is force-stopped, the system shuts its service down and binds it again, which starts the process and with it the recovery (`VoiceInteractionManagerService.java:3267-3277`: "The user is force stopping our current interactor, restart the service."). Not tried on the phone.
   - Whether the radio link really stays up after the process died is Android's decision and [U] for this phone. Android's source has two ways to run the voice link (A4.b). Where the Bluetooth service holds the link itself, nothing drops it until a stop, a tap from the aid, a call or the next start. Where the audio service owns the link ("SCO managed by audio": `HeadsetService.java:903-913`, `:976-978`, `:2183`; `HeadsetSystemInterface.java:388-398`, `:415-435`), the source alone does not settle it. One test on the phone does (section 5.5).
   - Ways out that Android's source gives, none tried on the phone: a tap on the aid ends a stale recognition at Bluetooth level without the app (`HeadsetService.java:1737-1783`, `:1643-1659`), so the wearer has a way out by hand; an incoming call clears it too (`:1823-1826`).
2. **Android keeps the audio up after `stopVoiceRecognition`.** The app waits at most 1.5 s (`awaitLinkDown`, `audio/SessionService.kt:332-344`, polling every 20 ms), journals `session_link_down` with `down=false`, declares itself free and never looks again. If Android kept the link up, only the wearer would notice.
   - Skeptic: the line is not even always written. If the hands-free service object is gone at that moment the function returns before it (`:333`).
   - By Android's source a false return of `stopVoiceRecognition` mostly means the stack had ended the recognition already, which is harmless. The case that matters is a true return after which the audio stays up, and the app would not notice that.
3. **A write that never returns inside `close()`**, before the stop (R5). Theoretical.

Bounded per session but not in sum: presses that keep coming chain sessions of up to about 315 s each (R1).

### 4.5 What else the app does to audio

None of this is a command to the aid. It is what the phone does around a channel.

- **Volume:** the app sets none. (True for the code that was audited. A later build of the same day can set the phone's media volume around an announcement, a gain in the phone and no command to the aid; see [announce-volume.md](announce-volume.md).) It reads the call volume for the journal (`audio/AudioLink.kt:120-121`). Tones have a fixed digital level (0.35 of full scale, `audio/Tones.kt:16`; the chime 0.25, `announce/Announcer.kt:258`) and play at whatever call or media volume the phone has.
- **Audio mode:** never set; read to keep presses and reminders out of a call (`input/InputRouter.kt:177`, `calendar/CalendarReminders.kt:272`). Android itself may change the mode while a voice link is up.
- **Audio focus in a session:** `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT_EXCLUSIVE` with usage voice communication (`audio/AudioLink.kt:130-143`). Other apps' playback, for example a podcast streamed to the aid, pauses for the whole session; released in `close()`.
- **Audio focus in an announcement:** `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT_MAY_DUCK` with usage assistant (`announce/Announcer.kt:118-121`). Other media is ducked, or paused if the other app chooses to, for up to 20.78 s; released in a `finally`.
- **Media during a session:** [V] *overstated*, corrected here. The audit wrote that the Bluetooth stack suspends the media stream while the voice link is up, so anything streamed to the aid stops for the length of a session. That is true for one of two branches only (A4.f). Whether media stops during a session on this phone is not shown by the source.
- **Communication route:** `setCommunicationDevice` only in the fallback P9, cleared in `close()`; `clearCommunicationDevice` once more in the recovery.
- **Microphone uplink:** a voice link has two directions, so the aid's microphone signal is on air for the whole session. The app reads it only in the echo test (`audio/AudioLink.kt:205-250`, 4 s).
- **Media keys:** none handled. The manifest declares `SET_MEDIA_KEY_LISTENER` but no code uses it; there is no `MediaSession`.
- **An unanswered tap:** a tap that meets a busy service or a refused start gets no answer; by [`bt-audio.md`](bt-audio.md) (section 5.5) Android reports a failure to the aid after 5 s. Whether the aid then plays a tone of its own is unknown.
- **Radio sharing** [I], unmeasured: during a session the BLE scan for the remote runs in its session mode on the same 2.4 GHz radio as the voice link. That could affect the quality of the stream, not the aid's own amplification.

### 4.6 What does not touch the aid's audio

All [V] from the code, and for the proxy and the scan also from the phone's side (A8.a, A8.b).

- The all-day BLE scan for the remote (`shelly/ListenerService.kt`, `shelly/RemoteScan.kt`, the rescue scan, the search scan on the screen): it only receives advertisements of the remote. No connection to the aid, no audio API.
- Holding the hands-free profile proxy for the life of the process (`audio/HeadsetLink.kt:36`): a handle to Android's Bluetooth service, used to ask which devices are connected and whether audio is up. No audio link exists until `startVoiceRecognition` is called. The app holds no A2DP proxy at all.
- The permanent foreground service of the listener (type `connectedDevice`) and its notification. The session service (type `microphone`) exists only while a session runs. Both notification channels are silent (A7.e).
- Making the spoken phrases (`speech/PromptCache.kt`, `speech/SpeechEngine.kt:63`): `synthesizeToFile` writes sound files, nothing is played.
- The announcer's speech engine object, kept for the life of the process after its first use: a service binding that is idle between announcements.
- Calendar planning: reading the calendar storage, the one alarm, the change observer, the retry every 20 s during a session or call. Only a due and permitted reminder reaches the announcer (P2).
- An announcement that is skipped by its checks (no aid selected, aid not connected, output not the aid alone, no engine, session or call for a reminder, alarm too late): the checks come before the first sample.
- A press that is dropped (phone call, older than 20 s, "Shelly weckt auf" off, unknown gesture) and a session Android refuses to start: a journal line and at most a vibration of the phone.
- Vibration on failures: the phone's own motor.
- Wake locks: they keep the processor awake and have no part in audio.
- The journal and the screen: read-only questions about power, lock, battery, role and whether the aid is connected.
- The adb commands `lara.STATUS`, `lara.SET` and `lara.MARK`. The others go through the router and are P5 to P8; `lara.CALENDAR` can speak a due reminder (P2).

### 4.7 What the journal can show at commit `cfaccde`

Every line carries wall time and elapsed time, so differences between lines are exact.

- **Voice link, recorded.** Open: `session_start`, `session_voice_recognition_requested` with `accepted`, `session_hfp_audio_up` or `session_hfp_audio_timeout`, `session_link_up` with `method`, and Android's own report `hfp_audio_state`. Close: `session_end` with `result` and `voiceRecognitionStopped`, then `session_link_down` with `down` and `waitedMs`, sometimes `hfp_audio_state state=disconnected`. Per session the seconds the link was up can be computed to about 20 ms: the time of `session_link_down` (`down=true`) minus the time of `session_hfp_audio_up`. The app does not add them up; the counters on the screen count sessions by result, not seconds.
- **Voice link, missing.** No session id (lines have to be paired by order, which works because sessions never overlap). With `down=false` the end is unknown. After a process death there is no closing line at all. No `session_link_down` line when the hands-free service object is gone. The `ms` field of the session's lines counts from the creation of the link object, not from the press or tap. `hfp_audio_state` is journaled only while `SessionService` exists, so the final "disconnected" may be missed and voice-link audio outside a session (a phone call) is not in the journal.
- **Media channel, recorded.** `announce_alarm`, `calendar_alarm`, `calendar_announce`, then `announce_start` (right before the chime), `announce_speaking`, `announce_end` with `result`, and `announce_skipped` or `announce_error`. That is the app's own window, a lower bound. The `ms` in `announce_end` counts from the start of the checks, so use the line times. The chime has no line of its own (fixed 730 ms); a chime without speech shows only as `announce_start` and `announce_end` with no `announce_speaking` between them.
- **Media channel, missing.** No line says when the A2DP stream to the aid really started or stopped. Android's 3 s tail, the possible stream after a session (3.6) and streams opened by the phone's own sounds (notification, touch and lock sounds open the same stream) are invisible. So the journal cannot tell the app's share from the phone's.
- **Never in the journal:** what the aid itself did (which program, how loud the surroundings). This app cannot observe it.

What closes the gap is in section 3.8 (A6.a to A6.g): permanent journaling of the aid's A2DP playing state and of its hands-free audio state from the listener service, a session id, a follow-up check when a link does not go down, and day sums of seconds per channel.

## 5. What only the ear can tell, and the tests that settle it

No phone and no aid were at hand for this report. Nothing below has been done.

### 5.1 What no document and no journal can answer

1. How much quieter, and how differently processed, the surroundings are during an announcement and during a session, with this aid and this fitting.
2. How many seconds the EON R stays out of its hearing program each time, beyond the time the stream is open.
3. Which program a voice-recognition link selects (H2.4), and whether "voice link closed, media stream opened" counts as one switch or two for the aid (3.7).
4. Whether a stream of pure silence already makes the aid switch, so whether the 3 s tail costs hearing time at all.
5. Whether the aid plays tones of its own at the start or the end, and whether it reacts to the error result of R11.
6. Whether unsaved adjustments and the Ambient balance position survive an announcement (H3.2, H3.3).
7. Whether setting the microphone attenuation to 0 dB removes a dip, if there is one (H1.5).
8. Whether Bluetooth trouble with this phone triggers the mute-or-restart cycle of H8.1.

### 5.2 By ear

1. **Announcement against a steady voice.** Sit two metres from a radio with a talk programme at normal room volume, or let someone read aloud at a steady level. Trigger one announcement from the app's screen; repeat three times. Notice: does the radio get quieter, duller, or louder and noisier the moment the chime starts; can the words still be followed during the announcement; count the seconds from the last spoken word until the radio sounds as before.
2. **Voice session against the same voice.** Same set-up. Open a session with a double tap and let it run its 30 seconds with one or two presses on the remote. Notice: is the radio quieter than in test 1, the same, or unchanged from normal; does one's own voice sound different; how long after "Beende" until the room sounds normal. While the session runs, look at myPhonak: which program name does it show (settles H2.4).
3. **Announcement in noise.** Make steady noise (kitchen fan or running tap plus the radio), wait two minutes so the aid has settled, then trigger an announcement while someone is talking. Notice: does the noise jump up or the voice get harder to follow during the announcement; afterwards, is the noise at once as before, or does it take some seconds to calm down (H4.2, H4.3).
4. **Tones from the aid itself.** In a quiet room trigger three announcements with a minute between them. Notice: any beep or short melody that is not the app's chime, before the chime or some seconds after the last word. Then, during a longer stream (a podcast), press the upper and the lower part of the button on the aid: does the room get quieter or louder against the podcast, and does a beep mark the end of the range.
5. **What is remembered.** Play a podcast, open myPhonak, note the position of the slider "Umgebungsbalance", move it fully toward the surroundings, stop the podcast. Trigger an announcement, start the podcast again and look at the slider. Separately: in the normal program raise the volume two steps in myPhonak without saving, trigger an announcement, look at the volume again.
6. **The way out.** Once, on purpose, switch "Bluetooth streaming" off in myPhonak (Devices, Streaming via Bluetooth) and trigger an announcement; switch it on again afterwards. With streaming off the aid must keep hearing normally and stay silent for the app. Also practise the 15-second button press for a reboot: afterwards the start melody and the room as before.

### 5.4 Once, over Wi-Fi debugging

These settle unknowns of section 3.12 without the ear. None was run.

- `adb shell getprop ro.audio.flinger_standbytime_ms` and `adb shell dumpsys media.audio_flinger | grep -i 'standby delay'`: the standby delay per output (A1.a, A1.i).
- `adb shell getprop | grep -i -e a2dp_offload -e sco` and `adb shell getprop bluetooth.sco.managed_by_audio`; the flag line in `adb shell dumpsys audio`; logcat for "isScoManagedByAudioEnabled state is": offload, and which of the two ways runs the voice link (A4.b).
- `adb logcat -s APM_AudioPolicyManager` right after a session end: "getNewOutputDevices io <n> recent device override <devices>" names the device the session's output was moved to (3.6).
- `adb logcat` for `HeadsetStateMachine` after a second tap: the error result of R11.

### 5.5 A process death inside a session

Start a session, then end the app's process with `adb shell am crash io.github.domicc.lara` or `am stop-app`. Not `am kill`: by the skeptic's recollection of Android's adb documentation (not opened on that day) it does not kill a process that has a foreground service. Then read the hands-free audio state in logcat (`HeadsetService`, `HeadsetStateMachine`), listen, and look for `process_start` and `session_recovered_after_crash` in the journal. This shows whether the radio link survives a dead app on this Pixel, and how long it takes until the recovery runs. Repeat with a force stop from the settings, to see whether the assistant service is bound again as the source says (4.4).
